## Design Context

### Users
People who arrive from a LinkedIn post or a shared link, mostly on phones and mostly curious rather than expert. They want to find their own country's "type", laugh at the archetype names, compare lenses, and screenshot something to share. A smaller group (policy and constitutional-law people) explores clusters on desktop.

### Brand Personality
Deadpan and scholarly about a silly premise: "an unserious typology of 193 serious documents". It should be witty, civic and editorial, not a dashboard and not a quiz app.

### Aesthetic Direction
Civic editorial. Warm paper background, ink-dark text, and a single seal-red accent. Serif display type (Libre Caslon, the Caslon tradition used to print founding documents) paired with Public Sans (a civic, government-design-system sans) for the UI. Hairline and double rules in the manner of legal documents. Lenses are framed as "Articles". No cards, side-stripes, glow or gradients. There are no brand constraints from the owner: palette and fonts are open.

### Design Principles
1. The map is the hero. Everything else explains or filters it.
2. Every hover has a tap equivalent. Phones are first-class.
3. Colour encodes MBTI type (E warm / I cool, N vivid / S muted), and the page always says so.
4. Reference material (pole definitions) is available but collapsed; archetypes come first.
5. Self-contained single HTML file generated by `src/visualize.py`.
